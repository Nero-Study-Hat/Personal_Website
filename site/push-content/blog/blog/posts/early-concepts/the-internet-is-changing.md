The internet has been going through an incredible amount of change recently and this is set to continue for some time as more significant developments are projected to follow.

This article is going to be organized by the different fronts on which the internet is changing
1. The model: Client-Server to Client-Edge-Server
2. The task: WWW and Communication to Infrastructure and Everything Goal
3. The participant: Human to Bot
4. The danger: AI Enablement and Nation State Incentives

My goal here is to provide a helpful overview of what is currently going on and what it may mean for the future.
## Client-Server to Client-Edge-Server

For a long time digital services have been provided in a client server model where the client is a user's personally owned device and the server is a computer controlled by the service provider.

Clients often
- are not trusted by the service provider, treated as potentially under malicious control
- are most trusted by the user, what is on their device is what they own and private to them
- have low hardware resources, programs try to use as little as possible on the client
- are not standard, all kinds of different client devices may exist to work with
- are sometimes powered on

Servers on the other hand often
- are trusted by the service provider
- are less trusted by the user
- have more hardware resources
- are standard
- are always powered on

This model worked well for a longtime as small personal computers spread which relied on far off servers to use various services. The main changes that is pushing the limits of this model are
1. The amount of devices has been exponentially rising upward with the rise of Internet of Things (IoT) which is going further into the Internet of Everything (IoE).
2. The functionality and resources of many of these devices are extremely limited with many being dumb data collectors without any intelligence built in and hardware to match this.
3. The hardware and software surface area to defend from attacks increases dramatically with all these new devices and the accompanying software.

In the new Client Edge Server model the edge is often
- service provider owned hardware close to the user
- very functionality and resource light, often some sort of data collector
- a bunch of grouped together devices meant to operate together in one network as opposed to on one circuit board

Demands from these changes will include faster speeds, handling of higher loads, protection of more surface area, resilience to greater DDoS attacks, improvement of local network performance for device clusters, greater data synthesis and utilization capabilities for the increasing data volume, private network backbones, additional internet area coverage, increased internet connectivity infrastructure resiliency to physical incident, etc.

Additional changes of note will include increased traffic obfuscation through compromised devices, increased surveillance from all origins, more dumb internet connected devices being manufactured which will reach EOL producing many more vulnerable devices that are cost effectively non-remediable, further sprawl of shadow IT (use of IT software or hardware without knowledge of what's in use), etc.

While I can find more areas of increased demand and changes of note the thing I want to focus on now is how to translate the impact of these things for people not in the info-tech space.

There are really three key areas here. What will people have to pay for the future, what risks are posed to them, and what benefits will come of this.

People are likely to pay for
- device clusters as opposed to singular devices
- integration of different devices in these clusters
- specialized maintenance of more devices
- connections to central servers, sometimes on a per device within cluster basic
